# Element 9: Monitoring, reporting and maintaining client relationships

This element covers the work after the trade. It carries about **7 of the 120 questions**. Expect short scenarios. The only calculations are in 9.3, each a step or two on the on-screen calculator. Rule content comes mainly from IDPC Rules 3200, 3400, 3600 and 3800.

## 9.1 Monitoring and evaluating portfolio performance

Monitoring is a loop:

1. Compare the portfolio with the client's objectives and agreed asset mix.
2. Judge the results against the markets.
3. Rebalance, change holdings or update the KYC information.

A portfolio that was suitable on the day of purchase can move away from the client's needs.

### The market and the economy

**Judge results in relative terms.** An equity portfolio that lost 3% when its benchmark index fell 12% may be well managed. One that gained 6% when its benchmark rose 20% may not be.

**Watch for stale holdings.** Changes in these factors can make holdings or the asset mix stale:

- interest rates
- inflation
- the business cycle
- a sector's outlook

If weights move outside their ranges, rebalance (rebalancing: 6.1).

**Treat a general market change as a portfolio issue first.** As good practice, a general market or economic change starts a portfolio-level review, not a new KYC. IDPC 3402(2) does not list it as a trigger event. But a change in a particular security can be a trigger event for the accounts that hold it. This applies if the change could make the security or the account unsuitable (IDPC 3402(2)(iv)).

CIRO product due diligence guidance is about the dealer's monitoring of approved securities (IDPC 3301(1)(iii)), not client accounts. For a general market change, CIRO "generally would not consider it a change requiring reassessment of individual securities" (GN-3300-21-001). A change or new information about only a particular security or sector may require a reassessment.

If a market change changes the client's own situation, it becomes a client issue. An example is a job loss in a recession.

### Client needs and circumstances

| Life events | What they can change |
|---|---|
| Retirement, divorce, an inheritance, illness | Objectives, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk profile |

**Keep the KYC current.** The dealer must update KYC information "within a reasonable time after the Dealer Member becomes aware of a significant change" (IDPC 3209(3)). It must also review the suitability information at fixed intervals (IDPC 3209(4); see 1.7):

- at least every 36 months
- at least every 12 months for a managed or discretionary account

Some accounts are exempt from this cycle, for example an order-execution-only (OEO) account (IDPC 3208).

**Review after a trigger event.** For a retail client's account, the dealer must do two things within a reasonable time after a trigger event (IDPC 3402(2); list in 1.21):

- review the account and its holdings against all the IDPC 3402(1) criteria
- take reasonable steps on the result

The trigger events include a deposit of securities and a KYC change that could make the account unsuitable. Section 1.21 lists all five trigger events.

The review does not apply to an OEO or direct electronic access account (IDPC 3404(1)). It applies the full IDPC 3402(1) test. This test includes:

- the concentration of securities in the account
- the liquidity of those securities
- putting the client's interest first

Guidance says to do it "in a timely manner, based on the nature and circumstances of the event" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.4).

**What goes wrong.** With a stale KYC, every later suitability determination uses old facts. The client can hold an unsuitable portfolio. The dealer also cannot show compliance (see 9.5).

> **Exam trap:** A decision to do nothing is still a decision. Guidance applies the suitability criteria to trigger-event reviews, "including decisions or recommendations to continue to hold securities" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.2).

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Perpetua Lachance, 61, loses her job in a recession. She now expects to withdraw money within a year.
>
> **Question.** What must the dealer do?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - Her income, time horizon and liquidity needs changed. The dealer must update her KYC within a reasonable time (IDPC 3209(3)).
> - The change could make her holdings unsuitable. This is a trigger event, so the dealer must review the account (IDPC 3402(2)).
> - Any sale to raise cash must take its tax effect into account (see 9.2).

*Sources: IDPC 3208, 3209(3)–(4), 3301(1)(iii), 3402(1)–(2), 3404(1); CIRO GN-3300-21-001; CIRO GN-3400-21-004, ss. 3.4.2, 3.4.4; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 9.2 Performance disclosure and measurement

### How performance is measured

**Total return** is price change plus income. A **time-weighted return** removes the effect of client cash flows, so it judges the manager. A **money-weighted return** (an internal rate of return) reflects the timing and size of those cash flows. So it shows the client's own experience (5.8).

**The annual performance report.** A dealer must send an annual performance report to a retail client who meets all of these conditions (IDPC 3810(1)):

- the client has an account with a balance or positions, or has reportable outside holdings
- at least one position or outside holding has a market value that the dealer can determine
- the client opened the account at least 12 months ago

The dealer may send the first report within 24 months (IDPC 3810(4)(i)). The report gives combined figures for the client's account and outside holdings (IDPC 3810(2)). It shows:

- opening and closing market values
- deposits and withdrawals
- the change in market value
- the "annualized total percentage return calculated net of charges using a money weighted rate of return calculation methodology" (IDPC 3810(2))

The return periods are the past 12 months, 3, 5 and 10 years, and since July 15, 2015. For an account opened before that date, the last period can start at account opening if the data is available. The report must also explain the calculation method in plain language (IDPC 3810(2)(vi)).

**Fund return versus account return.** Fund standard performance data are unit-value (time-weighted) returns before sales charges (5.9; NI 81-102 s. 15.4). The IDPC 3810 return is money-weighted for the whole account, net of charges. So the two figures can differ.

### How comparative performance is determined

Compare the portfolio with two things:

- a **benchmark:** an index or a blend of indices
- a **peer group:** similar funds, often as a quartile rank (a first-quartile fund did better than at least 75% of its peers)

Use the same period and fee basis for both sides. Choose peers with the same investment style. These errors give a false rank:

- **Style mismatch.** A small-cap fund in a large-cap peer group looks good or bad because of its style, not skill.
- **Survivorship bias.** Peer groups often drop closed funds, which are usually poor performers. So the peer average is too high.
- **Gross against net.** A fund's return before fees against peer returns after fees makes the fund look better.

### The use of multi-factor regression

**Multi-factor regression** shows where return came from, factor by factor (models: 6.7; formula: 9.3). A fund that "beat the index" only with small-cap value stocks shows little true alpha after you include those factors.

### The proper use of benchmarks

Choose the benchmark in advance, and match it to the mandate (criteria: 5.9; blends: 4.10). If you judge a balanced fund only against an equity index, the comparison is misleading. If you choose the benchmark after you see the results, the results can look better than they are.

### Transaction costs and tax implications of investment recommendations

The suitability test includes "the potential and actual impact of costs on the retail client’s returns" (IDPC 3402(1)(i)(d)). Guidance gives cost a wide meaning that includes "trailing commissions" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.2.5). Transaction costs include commissions, spreads and switching charges. The test also requires "a consideration of a reasonable range of alternative actions" (IDPC 3402(1)(i)(e)).

In a non-registered account, a sale can realize a taxable capital gain, so after-tax return matters (tax: 7.10, 7.11). Guidance says to liquidate "being mindful of any tax or other consequences" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.3).

### Fees and charges for the product and the impact on client returns

The annual **fee/charge report** (IDPC 3811) includes, for example, these items:

- operating and transaction charges
- trailing commissions the dealer received
- for investment funds, fund expenses and each fund's expense ratio

If the dealer received trailing commissions on the client's securities, the report must include a notice. The notice says that these fees "reduce the amount of the fund’s return to you" (IDPC 3811(2)(ix)). Costs compound over time (illustration: 1.16).

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Clementine Oyelaran's account holds only one fund. The fund shows a 1-year return of 9%. Her annual performance report shows 6% for the same year. She invested most of her money just before a market fall.
>
> **Question.** Is one figure wrong?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - No. The fund figure is a unit-value return before sales charges, so her cash flows do not affect it (5.9).
> - Her report is money-weighted and net of charges (IDPC 3810(2)). More of her money was in the fund during the weak part of the year.

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Thibault Thorvaldsen holds a bond fund with a large unrealized gain in a non-registered account. His Registered Representative (RR) wants to switch him to a similar fund with a slightly lower expense ratio. The switch has a fee.
>
> **Question.** What must she consider?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - The switch fee and any commission, not only the lower expense ratio (IDPC 3402(1)(i)(d)).
> - The capital gain tax that the sale causes now (7.10).
> - The alternative: keep the current fund (IDPC 3402(1)(i)(e)).
> - She must document the reasonable basis for her conclusion (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.2.5).

*Sources: IDPC 3402(1)(i)(d)–(e), 3810, 3811; CIRO GN-3400-21-004, ss. 3.2.5, 3.4.3; NI 81-102 s. 15.4; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 9.3 Investment performance calculations

```
Holding-period return   HPR = (P₁ − P₀ + D) ÷ P₀
Linked (multi-period)   cumulative = (1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)…(1 + rₖ) − 1
Annualized              (1 + cumulative)^(1/t) − 1      (only if t > 1)
Standard deviation      σ = √[ Σ(Rᵢ − R̄)² ÷ (n − 1) ]
Sharpe ratio            (Rₚ − R_f) ÷ σₚ
Treynor ratio           (Rₚ − R_f) ÷ βₚ
Jensen's alpha          αₚ = Rₚ − [R_f + βₚ(R_m − R_f)]     (see 6.8)
Active return           Rₚ − R_benchmark
```

The symbols are:

- P₀, P₁ = value at the start and at the end; D = income
- rᵢ, Rᵢ = period returns; k = number of linked periods; t = number of years (k ÷ periods per year)
- R̄ = mean return; n = number of observations
- Rₚ = portfolio return; R_f = risk-free (T-bill) rate; R_m = market return
- σₚ = standard deviation (absolute or total risk); βₚ = beta (systematic risk) (6.3)

### Rate of return

Link returns over several periods. Then annualize them to compare periods of different length. Annualize only a period longer than one year. For a shorter period, report the cumulative return. When returns vary, the annualized (geometric) return is below the arithmetic average.

> **Worked example**
>
> Ambrose Pemberton-Hale buys at $40 and receives a $1 dividend. The year-end price is $43. HPR = (43 − 40 + 1) ÷ 40 = **10%**.
>
> Two annual returns, +20% then −10%: 1.20 × 0.90 − 1 = **8%** cumulative. With t = 2, √1.08 − 1 = **3.92%** a year, not the 5% arithmetic average.

### Absolute risk / standard deviation

Standard deviation measures how widely returns spread around their mean. It is "absolute" because it uses no benchmark. Here, σ uses the sample convention (n − 1); 6.3 describes the population form (÷ n).

> **Worked example**
>
> A fund returns 6%, 10%, 14%. Mean 10%; squared deviations 16 + 0 + 16 = 32; 32 ÷ 2 = 16; σ = √16 = **4%**. With the population convention, √(32 ÷ 3) = 3.27%. Check which convention the question intends.

### Risk-adjusted returns: Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen

Each measure adjusts the return above the risk-free rate for the risk taken.

> **Worked example**
>
> R_f = 3%, R_m = 8%, market σ = 12%.
>
> Fund A: 10%, σ 15%, β 1.2. Sharpe = 7 ÷ 15 = **0.47**; Treynor = 7 ÷ 1.2 = **5.83** (percentage points of excess return per unit of β); α = 10 − (3 + 1.2 × 5) = **+1.0%**.
>
> Fund B: 9%, σ 20%, β 0.8. Sharpe = 6 ÷ 20 = **0.30**; Treynor = 6 ÷ 0.8 = **7.50**; α = 9 − (3 + 0.8 × 5) = **+2.0%**.
>
> Market: Sharpe 5 ÷ 12 = 0.42; Treynor 5 ÷ 1 = 5.0 (market β = 1 by definition).
>
> B has a higher Treynor ratio and alpha than A. But B's Sharpe ratio is lower than the market's. Its high σ relative to β signals company-specific risk. As a client's only fund, prefer A (Sharpe). As one small part of a diversified portfolio, prefer B (Treynor, alpha).

| Measure | Risk used | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Sharpe | Total risk (σ) | A whole portfolio or a client's only fund |
| Treynor | Systematic risk (β) | One part of a diversified portfolio |
| Jensen's alpha | β via CAPM | Return above or below the CAPM-required return |

### Performance against benchmarks

A fund earns 7% against a 60/40 benchmark (equities 8%, bonds 3%; blends: 4.10). The benchmark returns 0.6 × 8 + 0.4 × 3 = 6%, so the fund added **+1%** of active (relative) return. "Excess return" usually means Rₚ − R_f. Some texts use it for Rₚ − R_benchmark, so check the question's definition.

### Multi-factor regression

Multi-factor regression extends alpha. The Fama–French three-factor form is:

```
Rₚ − R_f = α + β₁(R_m − R_f) + β₂·SMB + β₃·HML + ε
```

SMB is the size factor and HML is the value factor. α is the return the factors do not explain. It is an estimate of skill, subject to luck and model choice. The loadings show the style.

> **Exam trap:** Rank by Sharpe, Treynor or alpha only over the same period and risk-free rate. A negative Sharpe ratio means the fund returned less than T-bills.

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Kazimierz Weber will move all his money into one of two equity funds. Fund X has a higher Treynor ratio but a lower Sharpe ratio than Fund Y over the same period.
>
> **Question.** Which measure fits his choice?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - Sharpe. The chosen fund will be his whole portfolio, so he bears its total risk, including company-specific risk.
> - Treynor counts only systematic risk, so it ignores risk that Kazimierz actually bears.

*Sources: standard finance concept (verified)*

## 9.4 Communicating with clients and the public

### Awareness of obligations and best practices

Communications shape what clients believe. This is one reason the dealer must supervise them. A dealer's policies must address communication with the public, and the dealer must monitor compliance (IDPC 3601(1)).

The IDPC defines three types of communication (IDPC 1201):

| Type | Meaning in short |
|---|---|
| Advertisement | Commercials, commentaries and published materials that promote the dealer's business |
| Sales literature | Material for client use with a recommendation on a security, derivative or trading strategy |
| Correspondence | A business communication prepared for a single current or prospective client |

Content and purpose decide the type, "rather than how they are distributed" (GN-3600-21-002). A designated Supervisor must pre-approve these items (IDPC 3602(3)):

- research reports and market letters
- telemarketing scripts
- promotional (not educational) seminar texts
- original advertisements or original template advertisements
- performance material used to solicit clients

The first use of a template advertisement needs pre-approval. Minor changes, such as the branch location, do not need approval (GN-3600-21-002). Guidance also says to check performance material against NI 81-102 (GN-3600-21-002; 5.9).

The dealer must review all other material by one of these methods, as the type of material requires (IDPC 3602(4)):

- pre-use approval
- post-use review
- post-use sampling

The dealer must keep copies and supervision records for the IDPC 3803 period (IDPC 3602(6)). No one should approve or supervise their own material (GN-3600-21-002).

**Public comments.** An employee or Approved Person may comment in public on the merits of an issuer or its securities. If so, they must disclose whether the dealer has issued a relevant research report (IDPC 3618(1)). An interview is also a public comment.

### The appropriate use of professional titles

Clients use titles to judge expertise. Under IDPC 3640(1), an Approved Person must not present themselves in a way that could reasonably mislead. A dealer must not present itself or its Approved Persons in that way, including through a trade name. The rule covers any of these matters:

- the Approved Person's proficiency, experience or qualifications
- the Approved Person's category of registration or approval
- the relationship, or potential relationship, with the dealer or the Approved Person
- the products or services provided now or in the future

> **Key rule:** An Approved Person who interacts with clients must not use, "if based partly or entirely on that Approved Person's sales activity or revenue generation, a title, designation, award, or recognition" (IDPC 3640(2)(i)).

The Approved Person also must not use these titles (IDPC 3640(2)(ii)–(iii)):

- a corporate officer title without an appointment
- a title that the dealer has not approved

IIROC Notice 14-0073 (CIRO guidance) asks dealers to examine seniors' or retirement titles carefully. It also gives criteria to check a designation, for example:

- a rigorous exam
- experience requirements
- ethics and continuing education
- a way to confirm the person's current status
- a public complaint and discipline process

### Misleading communications

In the IDPC, "Misleading communications" is the title of Part C, s. 3640 (see titles above). Separately, a dealer "must not issue, participate in or knowingly allow the use of its name in any advertisement, sales literature or correspondence that" (IDPC 3602(1)):

- is false or misleading, including by a material omission
- contains an "unjustified promise of specific results"
- uses unrepresentative statistics to suggest unwarranted or exaggerated conclusions, or does not identify the material assumptions behind them
- presents an opinion or forecast that is not clearly labelled as such
- fails to present the risks fairly
- is detrimental to the interests of the public, CIRO or its Dealer Members
- fails to comply with CIRO requirements or applicable laws

The harm comes before any trade: the client decides on a false picture of return, risk or skill. Records must show compliance with the misleading communications requirements, including IDPC 3640 (IDPC 3804(2)(xviii)).

### Restrictions and guidelines regarding social media and other communications with the public

If the dealer supervises the use and complies with the requirements (for example record retention), it may use social media for business. GN-3600-21-002 adds these points:

- **Static content** (profiles) is an original template advertisement that needs pre-approval.
- **Interactive forums** need supervision, not pre-approval.
- A recommendation on social media must take the Rule 3400 suitability requirements into account. Dealers should monitor or prohibit such recommendations.
- A like or a re-post may be an endorsement.
- Dealers should restrict anonymous chat-room posts.

Depending on the circumstances, registered firms that use finfluencers "may be held responsible for statements made on their behalf" (Staff Notice 31-369).

### Off-channel communication issues

CIRO's 2026 compliance report does not use the term "off-channel". It flags "client communications through non-approved channels" as a supervision gap. The report expects dealers to:

- "enforce strict controls over approved communication channels"
- monitor for non-approved platforms
- train their employees on approved communication practices

Under GN-3600-21-002, content decides whether a message is business-related, and the device is not relevant. A business-related message falls under the retention requirement on any device. The dealer must design its retention systems for the channels that it permits. So an uncaptured chat about a client's account is a retention and supervision risk. The dealer cannot use it to show compliance.

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Seraphine Taillefer, an RR, adds "President's Circle Advisor" to her LinkedIn profile. The dealer gives that award to its top revenue producers.
>
> **Question.** Is this allowed?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - No. The dealer bases the award on revenue generation (IDPC 3640(2)(i)).
> - Her profile is also static content, so it needs pre-approval (GN-3600-21-002).

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Radomir Volkov texts his RR on her personal messaging app and asks her to sell a holding. The dealer has not approved that app.
>
> **Question.** What must she do?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - The message is about his account, so it is a business record on any device (GN-3600-21-002). She must preserve it in the dealer's records, as the dealer's policy directs.
> - She should confirm the instruction through an approved channel before she acts.
> - She should tell Radomir to use approved channels. Guidance says dealers should discourage clients from sending orders by e-mail (GN-3600-21-002).

*Sources: IDPC 1201, 3601(1), 3602, 3618(1), 3640, 3804(2)(xviii); CIRO GN-3600-21-002; IIROC Notice 14-0073 (CIRO guidance); CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-369; CIRO Compliance Report 2026*

## 9.5 Maintaining client records

Records let the dealer prove what it did and why. The dealer must keep current records of its business and client transactions. The records must also "demonstrate the Dealer Member’s compliance with securities laws and Corporation requirements" (IDPC 3804(1)). The dealer's internal controls must give reasonable assurance that its records (IDPC 3804(3)):

- are correct
- give clear and accurate information
- stay current

> **Remember:** under IDPC 3804(2), records must:
> - demonstrate compliance with **KYC, account appropriateness, product due diligence, KYP and suitability** requirements
> - demonstrate compliance with **conflicts of interest** requirements
> - document the dealer's **sales practices, compensation arrangements and incentive practices**
> - document other compensation or incentive arrangements that benefit the **dealer, its Approved Persons, or an affiliate or associate**
> - demonstrate compliance with **misleading communications** requirements
> - document **correspondence with clients**

### Account appropriateness

Before the dealer opens an account, it must determine that two things are appropriate for the person (IDPC 3211(1)):

- the account itself
- the scope of products, services and account relationships in the account

It must decide "on a reasonable basis and putting the person’s interest first". OEO and direct electronic access accounts are exempt from the second test (IDPC 3211(2)). Some accounts are fully exempt, for example an account that a bank holds (IDPC 3211(3)).

Guidance says dealers must explain the features and costs of their account types, such as fee-based and commission-based accounts. They must recommend the type that puts the client's interest first (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.3.1). The documentation that supports the assessment is a client account record (IDPC 3210(1)(ii)).

### Know-your-client (KYC)

The "client account records" include these items (IDPC 3210; see 1.8):

- identity verification documentation
- account appropriateness documentation
- KYC information
- the account application

The dealer keeps them for at least **seven years** from creation, in durable and accessible form (IDPC 3220(3), 3803). Another requirement can set a different period. Within a reasonable time, the dealer must take reasonable steps to have the client confirm the accuracy of the KYC information (IDPC 3202(3)). That confirmation, KYC updates and periodic reviews (9.1) belong on file.

### Suitability determination

When they assess costs, Registered Individuals (RRs, Portfolio Managers and Associate Portfolio Managers) "must document the reasonable basis for their conclusions" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.2.5). The dealer also keeps evidence of supervisory reviews, including inquiries and their resolution (IDPC 3927(2)).

**Unsuitable client instructions.** If a retail client instructs an action that does not meet IDPC 3402(1), the dealer may act only after these steps (IDPC 3402(5)):

- tell the client why, and advise against the action
- recommend an alternative action that meets IDPC 3402(1)
- receive recorded confirmation of the client's instruction to proceed

### Know-your-product (KYP)

Product due diligence is the dealer's duty (IDPC 3301(1)). It should use "a documented approval process" (GN-3300-21-001). An approved product also needs documented post-approval follow-up, such as the monitoring of complaints and of sales restrictions.

Know-your-product is a separate duty. Before an Approved Person buys, sells or recommends a security, they must take steps to understand it (IDPC 3302(1)). Records must demonstrate compliance with both duties (IDPC 3804(2)(xii)). Shelf approval does not document the Approved Person's KYP (see 1.15–1.16).

### Conflict of interest

Staff Notice 31-363 sets no required format. But dealers must document how they identified and assessed each conflict. They must also document whether each conflict is material. For each material conflict, they must document the controls that address it in the client's best interest. The notice suggests a conflicts inventory that the dealer reviews at least once a year. Records need more detail as materiality increases (see 1.26).

Disclosure alone never satisfies the conflicts rule (IDPC 3113(3); see 1.23). So the dealer also needs controls. IIROC Notice 17-0093 (CIRO guidance) is older than the current rule, but it describes these controls. It names "firm-wide controls and/or documenting best-interest assessments" as specific measures.

### Sales practices, compensation arrangements and incentive practices

IDPC 3804(2)(xvii) covers the two groups of arrangements in the Remember box above. These records matter because pay can bias advice. A biased incentive creates a conflict of interest. Records must also demonstrate compliance with the conflicts of interest requirements (IDPC 3804(2)(xvi)). IIROC Notice 17-0093 (CIRO guidance) gives these findings.

**Sales practices.** Overly aggressive sales targets can cause inappropriate sales activity. So records should show the reason for each target and the controls on it.

**Compensation arrangements.** Records should show the grid and the dealer's review of it. Few dealers "can provide evidence of conducting a detailed review of their overall compensation program". The notice generally considers a product-neutral grid a best practice. A neutral grid gives no financial motive to prefer a product or an account type.

**Incentive practices and other compensation arrangements.** Most dealers paid the highest grid payout on fee-based revenue. Many also paid bonuses linked to fee-based assets. Many dealers could not show evidence that they monitored fee-based accounts.

**Incentives that benefit the dealer or the RR.** Clause (b) records cover these, including benefits to an affiliate or associate. An example is a higher payout for related-party products. Such an incentive magnifies a conflict, and does not address it.

> **Exam trap:** If the file does not show the reasons, the dealer cannot show that even a sound decision complied.

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** Bartholomew Ravensworth's grid pays more on fee-based revenue. He plans to move Graciela Almeida, a buy-and-hold client, to a fee-based account.
>
> **Question.** What must the dealer consider?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - The move must put Graciela's interest first (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.3.1; IDPC 3402(3)). A buy-and-hold client can pay ongoing fees without a matching level of service (IIROC Notice 17-0093). So the move is likely not appropriate.
> - A sound file cannot make an unsuitable move compliant.
> - If the move does suit her, the file must show why, including the cost (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.3.1). It must also show later monitoring (IIROC Notice 17-0093).
> - The file must show the dealer's review of the grid incentive as a compensation arrangement and a conflict (IDPC 3804(2)(xvi)–(xvii)).

> **Scenario**
>
> **Facts.** A client inherited shares and transferred them into her account. A supervisor queries the large position. The RR says by phone that the client will sell over time. Nobody writes anything down.
>
> **Question.** Is the review complete?
>
> **Resolution.**
> - No. The transfer-in was a trigger event (IDPC 3402(2)(i)). The supervisor should confirm that a suitability review covered the concentration and the sale plan (IDPC 3402(1)(i)(c); see 9.1).
> - The dealer must record and keep evidence of completed supervisory reviews, including the inquiry and its resolution (IDPC 3927(2)).

*Sources: IDPC 3202(3), 3210, 3211, 3220(3), 3301(1), 3302(1), 3402, 3803, 3804, 3927(2); CIRO GN-3400-21-004, ss. 3.2.5, 3.3.1; CIRO GN-3300-21-001; CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-363; IIROC Notice 17-0093 (CIRO guidance)*
