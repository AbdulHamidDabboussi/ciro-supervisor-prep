# Element 9: Monitoring, reporting and maintaining client relationships

This element covers what follows the trade: monitoring portfolios, measuring and reporting performance and costs, communicating with clients and the public, and keeping compliance records. It carries about **7 of the 120 questions**.

Expect short scenarios. The only calculations are in 9.3, each a step or two on the on-screen calculator. Rule content comes mainly from IDPC Rules 3200, 3400, 3600 and 3800 (plus 3927 on supervisory records), with CIRO guidance notes and CSA/CIRO staff notices.

## 9.1 Monitoring and evaluating portfolio performance

Monitoring is a loop: compare the portfolio with the client's objectives and agreed mix, judge results against the markets, then rebalance, change holdings or update the profile.

**The market and the economy.** An equity portfolio that lost 3% when its benchmark index fell 12% may be well managed; one that gained 6% when its benchmark rose 20% may not. Shifts in rates, inflation, the cycle or a sector's outlook can make holdings or the mix stale. Rebalance when weights drift outside their ranges (rebalancing: 6.1), or tilt tactically if the mandate allows. A market or economic change usually prompts a portfolio-level review rather than a new KYC; it becomes a client issue when it changes the client's own situation, such as a job loss in a recession.

**Client needs and circumstances.** Retirement, divorce, an inheritance or illness can change objectives, time horizon, liquidity needs or risk profile. The firm must update KYC information "within a reasonable time after the Dealer Member becomes aware of a significant change" (IDPC 3209(3); review cycle: 1.7). A trigger event under IDPC 3402(2) (list in 1.21) requires a review of the account and its existing holdings.

> **Exam trap:** Doing nothing is a decision. Guidance applies the suitability criteria to trigger-event reviews, "including decisions or recommendations to continue to hold securities" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.2).

*Sources: IDPC 3209(3), 3402(2); CIRO GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.2; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 9.2 Performance disclosure and measurement

**How performance is measured.** Total return is price change plus income. A **time-weighted return** strips out client cash flows (judging the manager); a **money-weighted return** (an internal rate of return) reflects their timing and size (the client's experience). Both are explained in 5.8. IDPC 3810 requires an annual **performance report** to retail clients: opening and closing market values, deposits and withdrawals, change in market value, and the "annualized total percentage return calculated net of charges using a money weighted rate of return calculation methodology" for the past 12 months, 3, 5 and 10 years, and since July 15, 2015 (or since account opening if the account is older and the data is available) (IDPC 3810(2)). It goes to accounts open at least 12 months; the first may come within 24 months (IDPC 3810(1)(iv), (4)(i)).

Note the contrast: fund standard performance data (5.9) are unit-value (time-weighted) returns before sales charges, whereas the IDPC 3810 client report is money-weighted and net of charges. A client's report can therefore differ from the fund's advertised return.

**Fees and charges.** The annual **fee/charge report** (IDPC 3811) totals operating and transaction charges, trailing commissions received and, for investment funds, fund expenses and each fund's expense ratio. Costs compound; see the illustration in 1.16.

**Transaction costs and taxes.** Suitability expressly weighs "the potential and actual impact of costs on the retail client's returns" (IDPC 3402(1)(i)(d)): commissions, spreads, switching charges. In a non-registered account a sale can realize taxable capital gains, so after-tax return matters; guidance says to liquidate "being mindful of any tax or other consequences" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.3).

**Comparative performance and benchmarks.** Compare with a **benchmark** (an index or blend of indices) and a **peer group** of similar funds (benchmark criteria: 5.9; blended benchmarks: 4.10). A balanced fund judged against an equity index alone is misleading.

**Multi-factor regression** shows where return came from (market, size, value; the Carhart model adds momentum; models: 6.7): a fund that "beat the index" only by holding small-cap value stocks shows little true alpha once those factors are included.

*Sources: IDPC 3402(1)(i)(d), 3810, 3811; CIRO GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.4.3; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 9.3 Investment performance calculations

```
Holding-period return   HPR = (P₁ − P₀ + D) ÷ P₀
Linked (multi-period)   cumulative = (1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)…(1 + rₖ) − 1
Annualized              (1 + cumulative)^(1/t) − 1
Standard deviation      σ = √[ Σ(Rᵢ − R̄)² ÷ (n − 1) ]
Sharpe ratio            (Rₚ − R_f) ÷ σₚ
Treynor ratio           (Rₚ − R_f) ÷ βₚ
Jensen's alpha          αₚ = Rₚ − [R_f + βₚ(R_m − R_f)]     (see 6.8)
Active return           Rₚ − R_benchmark
```

P₀, P₁ = starting and ending value; D = income; rᵢ, Rᵢ = period returns; k = number of linked periods; t = number of years (k ÷ periods per year); R̄ = mean return; n = number of observations; Rₚ = portfolio return; R_f = risk-free (T-bill) rate; R_m = market return; σₚ = standard deviation (absolute or total risk); βₚ = beta (systematic risk) (σ and β: 6.3; CAPM: 6.8). σ here uses the sample convention (n − 1); 6.3 describes the population form (÷ n).

> **Worked example**
> Leo buys at $40, receives a $1 dividend; year-end price $43. HPR = (43 − 40 + 1) ÷ 40 = **10%**.
> A fund returns 6%, 10%, 14%. Mean 10%; squared deviations 16 + 0 + 16 = 32; 32 ÷ 2 = 16; σ = √16 = **4%**. With the population convention (÷ n) the same data give √(32 ÷ 3) = 3.27%; check which the question intends.
> Two annual returns, +20% then −10%: 1.20 × 0.90 − 1 = **8%** cumulative; t = 2, so √1.08 − 1 = **3.92%** a year, not the 5% arithmetic average.

> **Worked example**
> R_f = 3%, R_m = 8%, market σ = 12%.
> Fund A: 10%, σ 15%, β 1.2. Sharpe = 7 ÷ 15 = **0.47**; Treynor = 7 ÷ 1.2 = **5.83** (percentage points of excess return per unit of β); α = 10 − (3 + 1.2 × 5) = **+1.0%**.
> Fund B: 9%, σ 20%, β 0.8. Sharpe = 6 ÷ 20 = **0.30**; Treynor = 6 ÷ 0.8 = **7.50**; α = 9 − (3 + 0.8 × 5) = **+2.0%**.
> Market: Sharpe 5 ÷ 12 = 0.42; Treynor 5 ÷ 1 = 5.0 (market β = 1 by definition).
> B wins on Treynor and alpha but trails the market on Sharpe: high σ relative to β signals company-specific risk. As a client's only fund, prefer A (Sharpe); as one small piece of a diversified portfolio, B (Treynor, alpha).

| Measure | Risk used | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Sharpe | Total risk (σ) | A whole portfolio or a client's only fund |
| Treynor | Systematic risk (β) | One part of a diversified portfolio |
| Jensen's alpha | β via CAPM | Return above or below the CAPM-required return |

**Against a benchmark** (blended as in 4.10): a fund earning 7% versus a 60/40 benchmark (equities 8%, bonds 3%: 0.6 × 8 + 0.4 × 3 = 6%) added **+1%** of active (relative) return. Some texts call Rₚ − R_f the "excess return", so check the question's definition. **Multi-factor regression** extends alpha (Fama–French three-factor form): Rₚ − R_f = α + β₁(R_m − R_f) + β₂·SMB + β₃·HML + ε (SMB = size factor, HML = value factor). α is the return the factors do not explain, an estimate of skill subject to luck and model choice; the loadings reveal style.

> **Exam trap:** Rank by Sharpe, Treynor or alpha only over the same period and risk-free rate. A negative Sharpe ratio means the fund trailed T-bills.

*Sources: standard finance concept (verified)*

## 9.4 Communicating with clients and the public

**Obligations.** A dealer must not issue, or lend its name to, advertising, sales literature or correspondence that is false or misleading, contains an "unjustified promise of specific results", uses unrepresentative statistics, presents unlabelled forecasts, or fails to present risks fairly (IDPC 3602(1)). A designated Supervisor pre-approves research reports, market letters, telemarketing scripts, promotional (not educational) seminar texts, original advertisements or original template advertisements, and performance material used to solicit clients (IDPC 3602(3)). Investment fund sales communications and standard performance data have their own rules (5.9). Other material gets pre-use approval, post-use review or sampling, and copies and supervision records are kept (3602(4), (6)). An employee or Approved Person commenting publicly on an issuer's merits must disclose whether the dealer has issued a relevant research report (IDPC 3618(1)).

**Titles and misleading holding-out.** IDPC 3640(1) bars holding out that could reasonably mislead about proficiency, qualifications or registration category, the relationship with the dealer or Approved Person, or the products and services offered.

> **Key rule:** An Approved Person who interacts with clients must not use, "if based partly or entirely on that Approved Person's sales activity or revenue generation, a title, designation, award, or recognition" (IDPC 3640(2)(i)).

Nor may they use an unappointed officer title or a dealer-unapproved title (IDPC 3640(2)(ii)–(iii)). IIROC Notice 14-0073 (CIRO guidance) asks firms to scrutinize seniors' or retirement titles and vet designations (exam, ethics, CE, discipline).

**Social media.** Business use is allowed if supervised and compliant (e.g. record retention). **Static content** (profiles) is an original template advertisement needing pre-approval; **interactive forums** need supervision, not pre-approval. Online recommendations trigger suitability; liking or re-posting may be an endorsement; anonymous chat-room posts should be restricted (GN-3600-21-002). Firms using finfluencers "may be held responsible for statements made on their behalf" (Staff Notice 31-369).

**Off-channel communications.** CIRO's 2026 compliance report, without using the term, flags "client communications through non-approved channels" as a supervision gap: dealers "should enforce strict controls over approved communication channels", monitor for non-approved platforms and train staff. Under GN-3600-21-002, content, not device, makes a message business-related; dealers must retain and retrieve permitted channels. So an uncaptured text chat about a client's account is a retention and supervision risk.

*Sources: IDPC 3602(1), (3), (4), (6); IDPC 3618(1); IDPC 3640; CIRO GN-3600-21-002; IIROC Notice 14-0073 (CIRO guidance); CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-369; CIRO Compliance Report 2026*

## 9.5 Maintaining client records

> **Remember:** under IDPC 3804(2), records must:
> - demonstrate compliance with **KYC, account appropriateness, product due diligence, KYP and suitability** requirements
> - demonstrate compliance with **conflicts of interest** requirements
> - document the dealer's **sales practices, compensation arrangements and incentive practices**, and other compensation or incentive arrangements benefiting the **dealer, its Approved Persons, or an affiliate or associate**
> - demonstrate compliance with **misleading communications** requirements

**Account and KYC records.** "Client account records" include documentation supporting identity verification and the account appropriateness assessment, KYC information and the account application (IDPC 3210; see 1.8). They are retained for at least **seven years** from creation, in durable and accessible form, unless another requirement sets a different period (IDPC 3220(3), 3803).

**Suitability.** When assessing costs, representatives "must document the reasonable basis for their conclusions" (GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.2.5). Evidence of supervisory reviews, including inquiries and their resolution, is kept too (IDPC 3927(2)). Records must also document client correspondence, complaint handling and temporary holds (3804(2)(xiii), (xiv), (xix); see 1.5, 1.6, 7.12).

**KYP and product due diligence.** The firm's product due diligence should run on "a documented approval process" (GN-3300-21-001). An approved product also needs a documented level of post-approval follow-up, such as monitoring complaints and compliance with sales restrictions. Separately, records must also demonstrate the Approved Person's own know-your-product compliance, which IDPC 3804(2)(xii) names alongside product due diligence; shelf approval does not document it (see 1.15–1.16).

**Conflicts and compensation.** No format is prescribed, but firms must document how each conflict was identified and assessed, whether it is material, and the controls addressing it in the client's best interest. Staff Notice 31-363 suggests a conflicts inventory reviewed at least annually, with more detail as materiality rises (see 1.26). On compensation, IIROC Notice 17-0093 (CIRO guidance) found few dealers "can provide evidence of conducting a detailed review of their overall compensation program", and many could not evidence monitoring of fee-based accounts. Example: if the grid pays more on fee-based revenue, the file should show the incentive was reviewed, why each move to a fee-based account suited the client, and ongoing monitoring.

> **Exam trap:** A sound suitability or conflict decision whose reasoning is not on file cannot be shown to comply.

*Sources: IDPC 3210, 3220(3), 3803, 3804(2), 3927(2); CIRO GN-3400-21-004, s. 3.2.5; CIRO GN-3300-21-001; CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-363; IIROC Notice 17-0093 (CIRO guidance)*
