# Element 8: Execution and market integrity

This element covers UMIR abusive-trading and gatekeeper rules, order types, settlement, cash and margin accounts, and client reporting: **7 of 120 questions** (indicative). Expect short scenarios (name the rule breached, match an order type to a client) with little calculation.

## 8.1 UMIR: best execution, abusive trading and front running

**Best execution** is "obtaining the most advantageous execution terms reasonably available under the circumstances" (IDPC 3119). Policies for listed securities weigh price, speed, certainty and overall cost (IDPC 3121), consider client instructions (IDPC 3123) and are disclosed in writing (IDPC 3129); OTC trades need fair pricing (IDPC 3122).

> **Exam trap:** Best execution is a policies-and-procedures duty, not the best price on every fill. A dealer "may not be able to achieve best execution for every single trade" (GN-URPART6-25-0003).

**Abusive trading.** UMIR 2.1(1)(a), 2.2 and 2.3 apply where the Participant "knows or ought reasonably to know", so a deliberate purpose is generally not needed. Cornering (Policy 2.2) and UMIR 2.1(1)(b) do require intent.

| UMIR | Prohibits | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1(1) | Specific unacceptable activities | Before the Rule 7.10 report is due, trading to fix a failed trade when the fix will itself fail |
| 2.2(1) | Manipulative or deceptive practices | Fictitious trades; wash trades (no change in beneficial ownership); cornering supply |
| 2.2(2) | A false appearance of trading or an artificial price | Matched orders; buying at successively higher prices; setting the close; orders not meant to execute (spoofing) |
| 2.3 | Improper orders and trades | Orders breaching securities law, SRO rules, marketplace rules or UMIR |

A price is **artificial** "if it is not justified by real demand or supply" (UMIR Policy 2.2 Part 3). Factors: surrounding trade prices, the price change caused, liquidity, timing, and motive.

**Front running.** A Participant that knows of a client order that "could reasonably be expected to affect the market price" must not, before that order is entered (UMIR 4.1(1)):

- enter a principal or non-client order in the security or a related security or derivative (e.g. calls);
- solicit others to trade;
- reveal it, except in the necessary course of business.

Exceptions (UMIR 4.1(2)): orders for the client's benefit or "solicited to facilitate the trade of the client order", prior hedges or binding obligations, arbitrage accounts, and no actual knowledge.

> **Exam trap:** Front running needs specific knowledge of an unentered client order: "Trading based on non-specific pieces of market information, including rumours, does not constitute frontrunning" (UMIR Policy 4.1).

*Sources: IDPC 3119–3129; GN-URPART6-25-0003; UMIR 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 4.1 and Policies*

## 8.2 Gatekeeper responsibilities

**Purpose:** employees act on "red flags" so abuse is caught early (GN-URPART10-26-0003).

> **Key rule:** An officer, director, partner or employee "shall forthwith report to their supervisor or the compliance department of the Participant" on becoming aware of account activity that may violate a listed rule (UMIR 10.16).

Listed rules include abusive trading, front running and best execution. Compliance investigates, records findings and reports violations to CIRO by the 15th day of the following month (UMIR 10.16(4)).

**Suspicious transactions.** Risk assessment considers "the client's characteristics, activities and transaction patterns" (GN-3200-23-001), so activity that departs from the client's usual pattern deserves a closer look (red flags: see 6.2).

Dealers report to FINTRAC any completed or attempted transaction where "there are reasonable grounds to suspect" money laundering, terrorist financing or sanctions evasion (PCMLTFA s. 7): "a step above simple suspicion", no dollar minimum, sent "as soon as practicable". Escalate to your supervisor or AML compliance (reporting to a superior is an employee's defence, s. 75(2)). Don't tip off the client (FINTRAC guidance); disclosing a report to prejudice a criminal investigation is an offence (s. 8).

**Insider trading.** In Ontario, a person "in a special relationship with an issuer" (insiders, employees, firms doing business with it, tippees) must not trade on an undisclosed material fact or change, tip it, or recommend a trade (OSA s. 76). Supervision must detect insider trading (IDPC 3945) (information barriers: 1.13).

| Reporting channel | Key features |
|---|---|
| Approved Person → dealer | Within two business days: own possible breach; a written client complaint against you; a complaint alleging, e.g., manipulation or insider trading against another Approved Person (IDPC 3702) |
| Dealer → CIRO | Client complaints (except service complaints), internal investigations and results, charges and proceedings (IDPC 3703(2)) |
| CIRO Whistleblower Service | Confidential "to the fullest extent possible"; no award |
| OSC Whistleblower Program | 5% to 15% of sanctions or voluntary payments (max $5 million) if the tip leads to over $1 million in sanctions; anonymous via a lawyer |
| OSA s. 121.6 | No reprisal for reporting to the employer, the OSC, a recognized SRO or law enforcement; clauses barring reports to the last three are void to that extent |

*Sources: UMIR 10.16; GN-URPART10-26-0003; GN-3200-23-001; PCMLTFA; STR Regulations; FINTRAC STR guidance; OSA ss. 76, 121.6; IDPC 3702, 3703, 3945; CIRO and OSC whistleblower pages*

## 8.3 Order types and client requirements

| Order | How it works | Suits a client who |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Buys at the best ask, or sells at the best bid, on entry (UMIR 1.1) | Wants certainty of execution |
| Limit | Buys at no more than a set maximum, or sells at no less than a set minimum: the limit or better (UMIR 1.1) | Wants price control; may not fill |
| Stop-loss / stop-limit | At the trigger price, becomes a market or limit order (sell stops sit below the market, buy stops above) | Wants to limit a loss or protect a gain |
| Immediate-or-cancel (IOC) | Fills what it can at once and cancels the rest | Accepts a partial fill now |
| Fill-or-kill (FOK) | Fills the whole quantity at once, or nothing | Wants all-or-nothing |
| Iceberg | Displays part of the size; a hidden reserve refills it | Has a large order and wants to limit market impact |
| Short sale | Sells a security the client does not own (UMIR 1.1) | Is bearish; the position is margined in a margin account (IDPC 5113) |

IOC, FOK and iceberg are market terms, not UMIR definitions. A triggered stop-loss with no limit "will become a 'market order'" and can fill far from the trigger price in a fast market. CIRO encourages limit prices on stop orders (GN-URPART7-25-0003).

**Short sales.** Short-sale order rules (UMIR 3.2 marking and borrowing, UMIR 3.3 settlement expectation) and margin: see 6.5.

> **Exam trap:** There is no tick test. The last-sale-price restriction on short sales was repealed effective October 15, 2012.

*Sources: UMIR 1.1, 3.2, 3.3; IDPC 5113; GN-URPART7-25-0003; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 8.4 Trade execution and settlement

**Marketplaces** are exchanges (e.g., TSX), quotation and trade reporting systems, alternative trading systems (ATSs) and internalizing dealers (NI 21-101). Trades go through "the entry of an order on a marketplace" unless exempt (UMIR 6.4).

**Orders, changes and errors.** The dealer records each order's terms "and of any modification or cancellation thereof" and its entry time (IDPC 3815). UMIR 6.4 permits adjusting "by a journal entry an error in connection with a client order". Supervision watches for "excessive transaction transfers and trade cancellations indicating possible unauthorized trading" (IDPC 3945).

**Settlement.** Most equity and long-term debt trades settle **T+1** (Companion Policy 24-101), meaning "the next business day following T" (NI 24-101). Government of Canada T-bills settle the same day (IDPC 4805). Delivery is normally book-entry through the clearing agency.

**Foreign exchange.** USD purchases from a CAD account convert at the dealer's rate, including a spread; a USD account avoids repeated conversions but not currency risk. CIRO recommends showing the exchange rate on confirmations of foreign fills (GN-3800-21-003).

**Cash account rule** (Form 1 Schedule 4 Note 7). Trades settle by payment or delivery on settlement date: cash, proceeds of securities held long in the cash account (equity permitting), or a transfer from an adequately margined account. If the account doesn't settle, "capital will be provided" by the dealer, so the rule limits its credit exposure.

> **Key rule:** When any money balance "is outstanding 20 business days or more after settlement date the customer shall be restricted from entering into any other transactions (other than liquidating transactions) in any account of the customer" (Note 7(vi)).

It lifts on payment, a liquidating trade that clears the overdue balance, or, for a client with no margin account, transfer of all open cash-account trades to a new margin account (Note 7(vii)). DAP (delivery against payment) accounts are restricted once a balance is 5 business days or more past the required DAP settlement date (15 for clients outside continental North America).

> **Exam trap:** For regular cash accounts the count starts at settlement date, not trade date.

*Sources: NI 21-101; UMIR 6.4; IDPC 3815, 3945, 4805; NI 24-101 and 24-101CP; GN-3800-21-003; Form 1 Schedule 4 Note 7; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 8.5 Margin accounts, special situations and trading authorizations

Margin rules keep client leverage appropriate (IDPC 5110). The client must be "aware of the risks and benefits associated with trading on margin" (IDPC 3246) and sign a margin agreement first (IDPC 3247).

| | Long margin | Short margin |
|---|---|---|
| Loan value | (100% − rate) × market value | (100% + rate) × market value, held as a credit |
| 1,000 shares at $20, 50% rate | Dealer lends $10,000; client puts up $10,000 | $20,000 proceeds + $10,000 deposit = $30,000 credit |
| Margin call if the price… | falls | rises |

Minimum client rates (dealers may set higher "house" rates) are set by price band, the same for long and short positions: at $2.00 or more, **30%** if on the List of Securities Eligible for Reduced Margin (LSERM), otherwise **50%**; then **60%**, **80%** and **100%** as the price falls; shorts below $0.25 are **$0.25 per share**. See the full IDPC 5310 table and the loan-value formulas in 6.5. To protect itself, the dealer may sell an indebted client's securities or buy back a short (IDPC 5116).

**Special margin situations:**

- control blocks are margined at 100% (IDPC 5340);
- concentrated positions may get reduced loan value (GN-5300-21-001);
- convertibles into LSERM stocks add any conversion loss to the reduced rate (LSERM page);
- registered-account debits fall under the cash rules, the margin rules or 100% margin (GN-FORM1-22-001);
- an account guarantee gives margin relief, but the guarantor is told that suitability "will not be reviewed in relation to the guarantor" (IDPC 5820).

**Trading authorizations:**

- The dealer keeps a signed trading authorization from the account holder for anyone else who gives instructions, plus a record of everyone with trading authority (IDPC 3220).
- Discretionary trading is allowed only in discretionary or managed accounts, apart from best-execution time-and-price discretion on a client order "for a specific amount or a specific security" (IDPC 3221(2)).
- A discretionary agreement needs designated-Supervisor approval, lasts at most 12 months and is not renewable (IDPC 3273–3274).
- A signed derivatives trading agreement comes before the first derivatives trade (IDPC 3251).

*Sources: IDPC 3220–3221, 3246–3247, 3251, 3273–3274, 5110, 5113, 5116, 5310, 5340, 5820, 5824; GN-5300-21-001; GN-FORM1-22-001; LSERM page*

## 8.6 Reporting to clients: confirmations and statements

The dealer must "promptly send the client a written confirmation of all purchases and sales" (IDPC 3816). It shows:

- trade day, marketplace (or accepted wording: "Traded on one or more marketplaces or markets, details available upon request") and settlement date; a separate average-price notice may be combined with it (GN-3800-21-003);
- quantity, description and consideration;
- regulatory fee, salesperson, and whether the dealer acted as principal or agent.

**Fees and commissions:** confirmations include "any commission charged in respect of the trade" (GN-3800-21-003, on IDPC 3816). For a retail client's debt trade that is not a primary market transaction, the confirmation shows either the total mark-up or mark-down, commission and service charges, or the commission plus a notice that dealer remuneration "has been added to the price of this security" (or deducted, on a sale) (IDPC 3816(2)(vi)(c); details, including the YTM and strip-yield items, in 2.3, 2.4 and 2.6). Retail confirmations for OTC non-debt securities show each charge and the total (IDPC 3816(2)(v)).

| Statement | Sent when (IDPC 3808, 3809) |
|---|---|
| Monthly | The client asks for it, or there was a transaction or a cash/position change (not just dividends or interest) in the month, or the account holds an open option, futures, forward or CFD position at month-end |
| Quarterly (minimum) | Any debit or credit balance, or securities, derivatives or bullion, held at quarter-end |
| Outside holdings report | Quarterly, to retail clients with outside holdings: off-book positions where the dealer is dealer of record (e.g. mutual funds held at the fund company) or receives continuing compensation |

Statements show opening and closing cash, deposits and withdrawals, and positions at market value (IDPC 3808), sent promptly (IDPC 3845).

> **Exam trap:** The dealer, not the Registered Representative, is responsible for confirmations and statements. Dormant accounts that still hold a cash balance or securities at quarter-end still get quarterly statements.

*Sources: IDPC 1201, 3808, 3809, 3816, 3845; GN-3800-21-003*
