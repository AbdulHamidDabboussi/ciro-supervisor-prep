# Element 1: Know-Your-Client (KYC) & suitability

This element covers the whole relationship between a Registered Representative (RR) and a retail client:
- **opening the account:** KYC, identity, the trusted contact person, account appropriateness, relationship disclosure and the welcome package;
- **recommending:** product due diligence, know-your-product and suitability;
- **maintaining the account:** updates, trigger events and supervision;
- **conduct:** conflicts, outside activities, personal financial dealings and the standards of conduct.

It is the largest element, with about **27 of the 120 questions**.

The content is rule-based and the syllabus asks for no calculations. Expect short client scenarios asking what the RR or the Investment Dealer must, may or must not do. Rules are cited by current IDPC numbers (August 2026 consolidation), with CIRO guidance notes, and NI 31-103 and its Companion Policy where the IDPC is silent.

**Terminology.** The syllabus's Investment Dealer is the IDPC's "Dealer Member" (IDPC 1201, where "Mutual Fund Dealer Members are to be excluded"); this guide also says "the firm" or "the dealer". An **Approved Person** is any individual CIRO approves "to carry out a function for a Dealer Member". The list includes the **Registered Representative (RR)**, an individual approved "to trade, or advise on trades, in securities or derivatives with the public in Canada, on the Dealer Member's behalf" (IDPC 1201).

## 1.1 The firm–client relationship

**Client relationship model (CRM).** The CRM is CIRO's set of client-facing obligations. CIRO guidance (GN-3100-21-001) says the CRM project addressed four elements:
- relationship disclosure (1.12);
- conflicts of interest management and disclosure (1.23);
- suitability assessment (1.17);
- account performance reporting (8.6, 9.2).

The four are designed to work together. The rationale CIRO gives for relationship disclosure (IDPC 3216) is that "all clients should have a good understanding of the services they will be provided when they open an account."

**Trust, agency and conflicts (concepts).**
- **Agent or principal:** for listed equities the dealer usually acts as **agent**, executing the client's order for a commission. For bonds and other over-the-counter products it usually acts as **principal**, selling from its own inventory at a markup or spread. An agent follows lawful instructions, acts loyally and accounts for the client's property.
- **Trust:** the client relies on the RR's competence and honesty, which is why regulation imposes KYC, suitability and conflict duties.
- **Conflicts:** the firm and RR are paid from the client's business, so IDPC 3110 requires them to identify material conflicts (1.23).

**Clients outside the RR's home jurisdiction.**

> **Key rule:** The Dealer Member must be "registered (or exempt from such registration) in the appropriate category under securities laws in each jurisdiction in which clients of the Dealer Member reside" (IDPC 2551(1)(i)). The same applies to the individual (2551(1)(ii)).

Dealing with a client while they are located in another country (for example, a U.S. resident or a "snowbird" wintering abroad) can engage that country's securities rules, which are outside this exam's sources. Follow the firm's cross-border policy and involve compliance.

**Change in residence.**
- **Client moves to another province or territory.** The NI 31-103 client mobility exemption lets an individual keep serving **5 or fewer** eligible clients there without registering (a firm, **10 or fewer**). An eligible client is someone who was a client immediately before moving, plus their spouse or child. Conditions include: the individual stays registered in their principal jurisdiction, with a registered sponsoring firm; the individual complies with Part 13 (dealing with clients); and, before first acting, the firm tells the client that the individual is exempt and "not subject to requirements otherwise applicable under local securities legislation." The sponsoring firm files Form 31-103F3 as soon as possible after first relying on the exemption.
- **Client leaves Canada (tax).** CRA treats the client as having disposed of certain property at fair market value: a deemed disposition, or "departure tax". Registered plans (RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, RESP) and pensions are excluded. The client may keep the TFSA, and withdrawals stay tax-free, but any contribution made while non-resident is taxed at 1% a month until it is withdrawn or the client becomes resident again. Non-resident RRSP withdrawals face 25% withholding unless a treaty reduces it.
- Either move is a significant KYC change and a suitability trigger (1.7, 1.21).

> **Exam trap:** The mobility exemption is triggered when the **client** moves, not the RR.

*Sources: GN-3100-21-001; IDPC 2551(1), 3110; NI 31-103 ss. 1.1, 2.2, 8.30; 31-103CP s. 2.2; CRA emigrant and departure-tax pages; CRA TFSA non-resident pages; CRA RRSP withholding page; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.2 KYC information required

IDPC 3202(1) requires the firm to "learn and remain informed of the essential facts relative to every order, account and client it accepts." The firm must:
1. establish the client's identity, and make reasonable inquiries into reputation if it has cause for concern;
2. establish whether the client is an insider of a publicly traded issuer;
3. collect sufficient information on six factors: **personal circumstances, financial circumstances, investment needs and objectives, investment knowledge, risk profile and time horizon**;
4. establish creditworthiness if it finances the client's purchase.

| KYC factor | What CIRO guidance includes |
|---|---|
| Personal | Age, address, family situation, dependants, employment, anyone else who can instruct on or has an interest in the account |
| Financial | Annual income, **liquidity needs**, financial assets, **net worth** (assets and liabilities), **borrowing or leverage to invest** |
| Needs and objectives | Liquidity; capital preservation, income, growth, or investing by personal values |
| Knowledge | Understanding of markets, the risks and limits of investment types, and the risk–return link |
| Risk profile | Tolerance and capacity (1.3) |
| Time horizon | Checked for feasibility against liquidity needs, age and objectives |

**Liquidity needs.** Ask whether the client has other means to cover expenses, whether needs are expected or unexpected, and whether withdrawals will be regular.

**Borrowing to invest.** Gather information on the client's ability to meet the debt obligations.

**Constraints and restrictions.** Guidance recognizes investing "in accordance with environmental, social and governance criteria or other personal preferences." The syllabus adds **equity, diversity and inclusion (EDI)** considerations, such as favouring issuers with diverse boards. No rule defines EDI, so record it as a client preference (portfolio impact of values-based constraints: 7.2).

> **Exam trap:** KYC must never be manipulated to match a product or to justify a pre-determined recommendation.

*Sources: IDPC 3202(1); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 2.2.5, 2.3.1–2.3.6*

## 1.3 Assessing the client's risk profile

Guidance splits the risk profile into "willingness to accept risk, sometimes referred to as risk tolerance, and ability to endure financial loss, sometimes referred to as risk capacity."

> **Key rule:** "The client's risk profile should reflect the lower of the client's assessed risk tolerance and risk capacity." (GN-3400-21-004, s. 2.3.5)

| Dimension | Question | Drivers |
|---|---|---|
| **Tolerance** (willingness) | How much loss and volatility will the client accept? | Preference, knowledge, experience; behavioural factors (concept; biases: 7.3) such as loss aversion, overconfidence, recent markets and question framing |
| **Capacity** (ability) | How much loss can the client absorb? | Financial situation, share of total investments at stake, current holdings, age and life stage, time horizon, liquidity needs |
| **Need** (concept) | How much risk is required to reach the goal? | Required rate of return, the market environment (low safe yields mean more risk for the same target), and the consequence of failure (an essential goal tolerates less shortfall risk) |

Risk need is a planning concept, not a rule. It can show that a goal is unrealistic, but it never lifts the profile above the lower of tolerance and capacity.

**A thorough process** assesses both tolerance and capacity, does not over-weight any single answer, and identifies clients better suited to cash or guaranteed products. Questions and answers are documented.

**Resolving conflicts.** When a client's return expectations exceed their profile:
1. discuss the relationship between risk and return;
2. never override the risk profile;
3. explain the alternatives (save more, wait longer, lower the target);
4. if the client lacks the capacity or tolerance, explain that the higher-risk portfolio is unsuitable;
5. document the discussion and the outcome.

> **Exam trap:** An eager 68-year-old whose savings must fund retirement has high tolerance but low capacity, so a **low** profile.

*Sources: GN-3400-21-004 s. 2.3.5; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.4 Business structures and investment opportunities

| Structure | Features | Impact on investing and the account |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietorship** | CRA: "An unincorporated business entirely owned by one person." The owner has unlimited liability (concept) | Business debts and cash needs are the owner's, so they shape the owner's capacity and liquidity |
| **Partnership** | CRA: two or more persons carrying on a business together and sharing profits or losses, usually under a partnership agreement. General partners have unlimited liability; limited partners risk only their investment (concept) | The firm must establish the partnership's existence and business and identify the individuals who control it (IDPC 3203). The agreement sets who may instruct |
| **Corporation** | Separate legal entity with limited liability ("Shareholders are not responsible for a corporation's debts"), continuous existence and separate tax; can issue shares or bonds. **Public:** generally a Canadian-resident corporation with shares listed on a designated Canadian exchange. **Private:** for example a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC), with no listed shares | The firm obtains the directors' names within 30 days and identifies holders of **25% or more** of voting rights (IDPC 3204). A separate account application uses the corporation's own KYC. Owners of a public company may be insiders, with trading restrictions |
| **Co-operative** | Member-based business. It may be incorporated federally under the Canada Cooperatives Act (filing with Corporations Canada) or under provincial co-operatives legislation (concept). It is democratically controlled, typically one member one vote, and returns surpluses to members (concept) | Member shares are usually illiquid; investment authority comes from the by-laws and the board |

CIPF generally treats a corporation or partnership as separate from its owners, with exceptions for certain personal holding entities (1.14).

*Sources: CRA business pages ("Sole proprietorships and partnerships", definitions, "Type of corporation"); ISED "Benefits of incorporating", "Co-operatives"; IDPC 3203, 3204; GN-3400-21-004 s. 2.7.2; CIPF coverage page; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.5 Documenting discussions and client confirmation

Within a reasonable time after collecting KYC, the firm "must take reasonable steps to have a client confirm the accuracy of such information" (IDPC 3202(3)).

> **Remember:**
> - Evidence of confirmation is the client's signature (handwritten, electronic or digital) or file notes of the client's instructions.
> - Verify changes in writing and give the client a chance to correct them.
> - Record the date of collection and keep evidence of the confirmation.
> - Document the risk-profile questions and answers, and any discussion of mismatched expectations.
> - Records must document correspondence with clients and demonstrate KYC and suitability compliance (IDPC 3804(2)(xii), (xiv)).

*Sources: IDPC 3202(3), 3804(2); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 2.3.5, 2.6.1*

## 1.6 Trusted contact person and temporary holds

**Definitions (IDPC 3201(3)).**
- A **trusted contact person (TCP)** is an individual named by the client, whom the firm may contact in accordance with the client's written consent.
- A **vulnerable client** "might have an illness, impairment, disability or aging-process limitation that places the client at risk of financial exploitation."

**Establishing a TCP.** Alongside the KYC, the firm must take reasonable steps to obtain the TCP's name and contact information and the client's written consent to contact them about four things (IDPC 3202(4)):
1. possible financial exploitation;
2. the client's mental capacity to make financial decisions;
3. the client's legal representative;
4. the client's contact information.

This applies to individual clients only. CSA guidance adds that a client may name more than one TCP, and that the TCP should not be the client's dealing or advising representative (the RR) on the account. A POA attorney may be named, though clients should be encouraged to choose someone not involved in account decisions.

**What a TCP may and may not do.** CIRO's investor brochure says a TCP "acts like an emergency contact for your account, although they cannot make financial decisions or account changes." A TCP does not replace a power of attorney (POA). The Relationship Disclosure must describe the circumstances in which the firm might disclose information about the client or the account to the TCP (3216(5)(ii)(n)).

**Refusal.** The account can still be opened, but the firm must take reasonable steps (explain the purpose, ask), may ask why, and keeps records showing compliance (NI 31-103 s. 11.5(2)(l)). It asks again at each KYC update.

**When to contact.** Contact the TCP if the client cannot be reached after repeated attempts, or on warning signs of:
- **exploitation:** sudden withdrawals, an unexplained jump to high risk, new "helpers", sudden POA or will changes, isolation;
- **lost capacity:** memory loss, trouble with forms, confusion over basic terms, missed appointments.

Speak to the client first. Never contact a TCP suspected of the exploitation; consider the police or the public guardian and trustee instead.

**Documenting the discussion.** Record conversations with the client and the TCP about exploitation or capacity concerns: what was raised, when, and the outcome. CSA expects TCP policies to cover "how to document discussions with a TCP", and records must document correspondence with clients (IDPC 3804(2)(xiv); NI 31-103 s. 11.5(2)(n)).

**Temporary holds.** A temporary hold is a hold on a purchase or sale, or on a withdrawal or transfer of cash or securities (IDPC 1201). It may be placed only if the firm reasonably believes **either**:
- the client is vulnerable **and** financial exploitation "has occurred, is occurring, has been attempted or will be attempted"; **or**
- the client lacks the mental capacity to make financial decisions (IDPC 3222(1)–(2)).

The firm must then (3222(3)):
1. document the facts and reasons;
2. notify the client, with reasons, as soon as possible;
3. review the hold frequently;
4. **within 30 days**, and every 30 days after, either revoke it or notify the client that it continues, with reasons.

Records must demonstrate compliance (3804(2)(xix)). CSA guidance says the hold should cover the specific transaction, not the whole account.

> **Exam trap:** A hold is not the tool for an unsuitable instruction; that is IDPC 3402(5) (1.18). A poor decision alone is not exploitation, and a hold may never be used to stall a withdrawal for fear of losing the client.

*Sources: IDPC 1201, 3201(3), 3202(4)–(5), 3216(5)(ii)(n)–(o), 3222, 3804(2)(xiv), (xix); NI 31-103 s. 11.5(2)(l), (n); 31-103CP Appendix G; CIRO brochure "Opening an Investment Account"*

## 1.7 Primary responsibility, no delegation, keeping KYC current

> **Key rule:** KYC compliance "is primarily the responsibility of the Registered Representative, Portfolio Manager or Associate Portfolio Manager assigned to the client account" and "must not be delegated to any other person" (IDPC 3209(1)–(2)).

- **Who is responsible.** The firm remains ultimately responsible. Suitability KYC may not come from an affiliate or an unregistered assistant, though identification information may. Any RR on an assigned team may collect it.
- **Keeping it current.** Update within a reasonable time after learning of a significant change (3209(3)). Significant changes include the risk profile, horizon, needs and objectives, or anything that could significantly affect income or net worth.
- **Review cycle** (3209(4)): at least every **36 months**; every **12 months** for managed and discretionary accounts. If nothing changes, keep evidence of the review.

*Sources: IDPC 3209; GN-3400-21-004 ss. 2.5, 2.11*

## 1.8 Client account records

IDPC 3210 defines **client account records** as information, disclosures and agreements the firm must give to or obtain from the client. They include identity verification, the account appropriateness assessment, KYC information and the account application.

| Record | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Identity verification** | Documentation that the client's identity was established (IDPC 3202(1)(i), 3210(1)(i)). For beneficial owners and controlling individuals of entity accounts, methods that let the firm "form a reasonable belief it knows the identity" of the individual, with a record of how it was confirmed (IDPC 3206) |
| **Appropriateness assessment** | Documentation supporting the 3211 determination (1.9) |
| **KYC information** | The 3202(1) information, confirmed by the client |
| **Acknowledgement of disclosures** | Records must evidence that required account documents were provided (3804(2)(xi)). Examples: positive acknowledgement of receipt of the leverage risk disclosure (3217(1)(ii)) and acknowledgement of receipt of the derivatives risk disclosure statement (3254(1)(ii)) |
| **TCP refusal** | No IDPC section names it; NI 31-103 requires records demonstrating compliance with the TCP section (s. 11.5(2)(l)) |

A designated Supervisor must not approve a new account until all client account records are collected (3214(2)). Approval is due no later than one business day after the initial trade (1.11). If the designated Supervisor does not approve the account after the initial trade, the firm must restrict it "to only liquidating trades, transfers out, paying out funds or delivering securities to the client" until final approval (3214(5)). Separately, the firm must take specific action on supporting documents still missing **25 business days** after opening (3213(2)(iii)). That is a follow-up deadline, not a window for approval.

*Sources: IDPC 3202(1)(i), 3206, 3210, 3213(2), 3214(2), (5), 3217, 3254, 3804(2)(xi); NI 31-103 s. 11.5(2)(l); 31-103CP Appendix G*

## 1.9 Account appropriateness

> **Key rule:** Before opening an account, the firm must determine, "on a reasonable basis and putting the person's interest first", that opening it is appropriate and that "the scope of products, services and account relationships which the person would have access to within the account are appropriate for the person" (IDPC 3211(1)).

**Applying it.**
- The assessment covers:
  - the type of service (OEO, full-service, managed);
  - account relationships (margin, options, futures, fee-based or commission-based);
  - the compensation option;
  - strategies such as leverage.
- The firm explains the features and costs of its account types and recommends the one that puts the client first. It need not consider other firms' accounts.
- If nothing it offers is appropriate, it should not open the account. If only some products are appropriate, it restricts access.

**Exemptions.**
- OEO and direct electronic access accounts: only the "may this person open this account here" question applies.
- Carrying-broker accounts and accounts of dealers, banks and insurers: exempt.

| Scenario | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Client wants to make every decision | Not a managed account |
| Client will rarely trade | Consider a commission-based account over a fee-based one |
| Novice with low capacity wants margin and options | Restrict those relationships |

*Sources: IDPC 3211; GN-3400-21-004 ss. 3.3.1, 3.3.3, 3.3.4*

## 1.10 Account types

**Definitions (IDPC 1201).**
- **Advisory account:** "the client is responsible for all investment decisions but is able to rely on advice given by a Registered Representative."
- **Managed account:** a Portfolio Manager or Associate Portfolio Manager makes decisions on a continuing basis.
- **Discretionary account:** the client grants unsolicited authority, accepted "to accommodate a client who is frequently or temporarily unavailable to authorize trades." It cannot be renewed and lasts no more than 12 months.

| Account | Advantages | Disadvantages | Key rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Advisory, commission** | Cheap for clients who trade rarely | Cost rises with activity; per-trade pay invites excessive trading | Suitability on every action |
| **Advisory, fee-based** (% of assets) | Predictable cost; aligns the RR with asset growth | A buy-and-hold client pays ongoing fees without matching service; payout grids often favour fee-based revenue | Fee vs commission choice must be appropriate at opening and on an ongoing basis |
| **Managed** | Continuous professional management | Client gives up control; fees | Written agreement describing KYC and restrictions; Supervisor approves in writing; fair-allocation policy; 12-month KYC review; volume- or performance-based fees need written agreement (IDPC 3277–3281) |
| **Discretionary** | Trading continues while the client is unavailable | Temporary only | Agreement of at most 12 months, not renewable; RR needs at least 2 years' active experience with all product types to be traded on discretion (3275(1)); designated Supervisor approves the account and the signed agreement (3273(1)(v)). No discretion elsewhere except time and price for best execution (3221) (8.5) |
| **Cash** | Simple; no borrowing | No leverage or shorting | Settle by payment or delivery on settlement date (Form 1, Sch. 4) (8.4) |
| **Margin** | Leverage; short selling | Magnified losses, interest, margin calls | Signed margin agreement before opening; client made aware of risks and benefits (3246–3247). Restrictions on an undermargined account (Form 1, Sch. 4, Note 5), loan values and rates: 6.5; margin accounts: 8.5 |

> **Exam trap:** Long-term portfolio management needs a **managed** account. Discretionary authority is a short-term, non-renewable accommodation.

*Sources: IDPC 1201, 3221, 3246, 3247, 3273–3281; IDPC Form 1 Schedule 4 Notes 5 and 7; IIROC Notice 17-0093; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.11 Account-opening documentation

**Depth of KYC.** The depth of KYC enquiries can vary with:
- the firm's business model;
- its relationships with clients;
- the securities and services it offers.

A fully customized service or complex finances call for more detail. Simple model portfolios for straightforward clients call for less. Illiquid or high-risk products may require information on holdings elsewhere. OEO, direct electronic access and institutional accounts need no suitability KYC, but they still need essential facts, anti-money-laundering information and creditworthiness where credit is given.

**Typical advisory package:** application with confirmed KYC, identity verification, TCP details and consent, Relationship Disclosure, conflicts and fee disclosure, complaint summary and brochure; where applicable, a margin agreement, risk disclosures, or a managed or discretionary agreement.

**Timing.**
- An account number is assigned only once the client's full name and address are known.
- The complete application is due by the next business day.
- Supervisor approval comes no later than one business day after the first trade (IDPC 3214).

**One set of KYC for several accounts** (for example cash, margin and RRSP) is permitted if:
1. the beneficial owner is identical;
2. the same RR or team handles all the accounts;
3. the firm can supervise them together;
4. the client acknowledges suitability may be assessed across them.

**Joint, corporate and trust accounts** need separate applications.

*Sources: IDPC 3208, 3214; GN-3400-21-004 ss. 2.3.7, 2.4, 2.7*

## 1.12 Relationship Disclosure

**Objective.** RDI is "a written communication from the Dealer Member to the client describing the products and services offered by the Dealer Member, the nature of the account and the manner in which the account will operate and the responsibilities of the Dealer Member to the client." It is required for **retail** clients only.

**Frequency.** At account opening, and when there is a significant change to the information.

**Form.** Customized for each client, or standardized by class of client (it must still fit the client). Several accounts may share a combined RDI if appropriate.

**Format.** In writing, in plain language, with all required content or references to where it is found. It may be stand-alone or part of the account-opening package.

> **Remember:** RDI content (IDPC 3216(5)) is titled "Relationship Disclosure" and covers:
> - products and services, including resale restrictions and fund ongoing fees, and any limits (such as proprietary products);
> - the account type and who decides;
> - the suitability process and its triggers, and whether market fluctuations trigger reviews;
> - account reporting (8.6, 9.2);
> - the conflicts statement;
> - third-party benefits;
> - account and transaction charges and their compounding impact;
> - account documents;
> - complaint procedures and brochure (7.12);
> - benchmarks (9.2);
> - when the firm might disclose client or account information to the TCP;
> - temporary holds.

OEO accounts omit the suitability description.

**Review.** A partner, Director, officer or designated Supervisor approves the RDI. For standardized documents, the designated Supervisor ensures the right version is used. If the RDI is customized for each client, the designated Supervisor must approve each document (3216(6)(i)).

*Sources: IDPC 3216; GN-3100-21-001*

## 1.13 Containment of confidential information

**Why it matters.** Dealers routinely acquire **material non-public information (MNPI)** through investment banking mandates, board seats, research contacts and insider clients. Trading on it or tipping it damages market integrity (insider trading prohibition: 8.2). CIRO says containment matters for every dealer, not only those doing investment banking.

> **Key rule:** A firm representative acting as an issuer's director, or in an underwriting or advisory capacity, and colleagues they properly tell, must not disclose MNPI "to anyone including any Directors, Executives, employees or clients, or research or trading departments of the Dealer Member unless in the necessary course of business" (IDPC 3508(2)–(4)).

**Information barriers (firewalls)** are "physical and procedural means of restricting access to confidential information to those needing to know it and of recording who gets access to it and when." They include an accountable senior officer, staff undertakings, physical and electronic barriers, and logged "wall-crossings".

| | Grey (watch) list | Restricted list |
|---|---|---|
| Contents | Issuers on which the firm holds confidential information | Issuers with which the firm has a **current, publicly disclosed** involvement |
| Circulation | Highly confidential, limited | Distributed to staff who take orders |
| Effect | Silent monitoring of trading for leaks | Restrictions on trading and advising |

**Departmental roles (concept, plus rules).**
- **Investment banking and corporate finance** raise capital and advise issuers. They are "private side".
- **Research** must stay independent. Analysts may not be paid based on a specific investment banking transaction (IDPC 3620(2)). Investment banking may not approve reports, may correct only factual errors, and gets no advance notice of rating changes (3621).
- **Retail RRs** are "public side".

**Cybersecurity.** A cybersecurity incident is unauthorized access to, or disruption or misuse of, the firm's systems that causes, or is reasonably likely to cause, substantial harm or other listed effects. It is reported to CIRO in writing **within three calendar days** of discovering the incident, with a follow-up report **within 30 calendar days** of discovery unless CIRO agrees otherwise (IDPC 3703(2)(vii)).

**Privacy.** PIPEDA governs private-sector collection, use and disclosure of personal information. Its ten principles are: (1) accountability; (2) identifying purposes; (3) consent; (4) limiting collection; (5) limiting use, disclosure and retention; (6) accuracy; (7) safeguards; (8) openness; (9) individual access; (10) challenging compliance. Breaches posing a real risk of significant harm must be reported to the Privacy Commissioner and the affected individuals notified.

> **Exam trap:** An RR who overhears that the firm is advising on a takeover must not trade, tip or hint. The issuer is likely on the grey list, which the RR cannot see. Report it to compliance.

*Sources: GN-3500-21-001; IDPC 3508, 3620, 3621, 3703; PIPEDA s. 3, s. 10.1, Schedule 1; OPC fair information principles; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.14 The welcome package

| Document | Purpose and requirement |
|---|---|
| **Fee schedule / charges** | The RDI describes all account fees and transaction charges (IDPC 3216(5)(ii)(h)–(i)). Before accepting a retail client's instruction to buy or sell (other than in a managed account), the firm discloses charges, any deferred sales charge, trailing commissions and fund ongoing fees (3218(1)) |
| **CIRO Complaints Brochure** | **Mandatory.** Explains how to complain to the firm and to CIRO; a copy is given at account opening (3724) |
| **How CIRO protects investors** | **Mandatory.** Explains that CIRO "regulates the activities of Canadian investment dealers and mutual fund dealers and the advisors they employ" |
| **Opening an Investment Account** | **Optional** investor guide: KYC questions, naming a TCP, questions to ask the advisor |
| **CIPF** | Firm discloses its membership, the fund's name and the coverage (IDPC 2284) |
| **Derivatives risk disclosure** | Warns that "you may lose more than the amount of funds deposited"; provided to each derivatives retail client before the initial derivatives order is accepted, and the firm obtains the client's acknowledgement of receipt (3254(1)(i)–(ii)) |
| **Conflicts disclosure** | Written, prominent, specific and in plain language, before opening if conflicts are already identified (3113) |
| **Complaint-handling procedures** | Written summary at opening and available on an ongoing basis (3724) (7.12) |

**CIPF (Canadian Investor Protection Fund).**
- **Covers:** client property (cash, securities and similar) held by a member firm that is missing when the firm becomes insolvent.
- **Does not cover:** market losses, unsuitable investments, misrepresentation, poor advice or issuer default.
- **Limits for an individual:** generally **$1 million** for general accounts combined (cash, margin, TFSA, FHSA), **plus $1 million** for registered retirement accounts (RRSP, RRIF, LIF), **plus $1 million** for RESPs.
- **Corporations and partnerships:** generally $1 million.
- **Claims** must be filed within 180 days of the insolvency.

For example, a client missing $1.2 million across margin and TFSA accounts is covered for $1 million of general-account property; an RRSP is covered under its own separate limit.

*Sources: IDPC 2284, 3113, 3216(5), 3218, 3254, 3724; CIRO Official Brochures page and brochures; GN-3200-24-001 Attachment A; CIPF coverage page*

## 1.15 Product due diligence (PDD)

> **Key rule:** A Dealer Member must not make securities available to clients unless it has taken reasonable steps to "assess the relevant aspects of the securities or derivatives, including the securities' or derivatives' structure, features, risks, initial and ongoing costs and the impact of those costs," approve them, and monitor them for significant changes (IDPC 3301(1)).

**On the firm.**
- A firm makes a security "available" by shelving it, buying it for a client, recommending it or advertising it.
- PDD can be **risk-based**: light for listed common shares, deeper for complex, novel, leveraged or prospectus-exempt products.
- The firm may restrict complex products, such as leveraged or inverse ETFs and principal-protected notes, to certain clients, or keep them off the shelf.
- It should do its own analysis rather than rely on issuer-commissioned reports.
- **Monitoring:** a change to the security itself is significant. A general market move usually is not, but a change specific to one security or sector may be.

**On the Approved Person.** The Approved Person "must not purchase securities or derivatives for, or recommend securities or derivatives to, a client unless the securities or derivatives have been approved by the Dealer Member" (3301(2)).

**Transfers-in and client-directed trades** generally need no shelf approval if the firm does not otherwise make the security available, but the firm must still take reasonable steps to assess them. PDD does not apply to carrying-broker arrangements.

> **Exam trap:** PDD is not underwriting due diligence. Underwriting due diligence concerns prospectus **disclosure**; PDD concerns whether a product fits the **client base**.

*Sources: IDPC 3301, 3303(1); GN-3300-21-001*

## 1.16 Know-your-product (KYP)

> **Key rule:** An Approved Person "must not purchase or sell securities or transact in derivatives for, or recommend securities or derivatives to, a client unless the Approved Person takes steps to understand the securities or derivatives, including the securities' or derivatives' structure, features, risks, initial and ongoing costs and the impact of those costs" (IDPC 3302(1)).

The steps required are those reasonable for suitability (3302(2)). **KYP is separate from PDD:** shelf approval does not discharge it. KYP covers everything in the account, including transfers-in and client-directed trades.

The RR should be able to explain:
- **Features:** returns, leverage, time horizon, complexity, conflicts.
- **Risks:** loss of principal, liquidity and redemption risk, embedded derivatives.
- **Costs:** commissions, sales charges, trailing commissions, management, incentive and referral fees.

**Impact of costs (concept).** Costs compound. $100,000 for 20 years at 6% before costs (annual compounding; net return = gross minus annual cost) grows to about $265,330 at a 1% cost but only about $219,112 at 2%.

**Don'ts.** Never recommend **solely** on issuer or third-party information, similarity to other products, or others' recommendations.

**Exemptions.** OEO and direct electronic access accounts, and carrying-broker arrangements.

*Sources: IDPC 3302, 3303(2); GN-3300-21-001; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.17 Retail suitability determination

> **Key rule:** Before taking or recommending any investment action, the firm must determine on a reasonable basis that it is suitable and "puts the retail client's interest first" (IDPC 3402(1)).

**The factors (3402(1)(i)):**
- **(a)** KYC;
- **(b)** the firm's PDD and the Approved Person's KYP;
- **(c)** the impact on the account, including **concentration** and **liquidity**;
- **(d)** the potential and actual **impact of costs**;
- **(e)** a **reasonable range of alternatives** available at the firm.

**How KYC drives the recommendation.**
- KYC, PDD and KYP come first, and a product's risk rating alone is not enough.
- Suitability is judged on the resulting **account portfolio**, not trade by trade (3402(4)).
- Several options may be suitable. The RR picks the one that puts the client first, ahead of higher pay, and discloses material positive and negative factors.
- CIRO judges the determination by what a reasonable RR would have done at the time, not with hindsight.

**Excessive switches (churning).** Churning is trading or switching mainly to generate commissions. The IDPC does not use the word, but retail supervision must detect "excessive trading" (IDPC 3945(2)(iii)).
- CSA guidance: before a switch, tell the client the charges, the dealer's incentives (for example higher trailing commissions) and the tax consequences, and explain why it is appropriate.
- CIRO's mutual fund dealer rules give red flags such as redemptions within 3 months of purchase or more than 5 trades a month.
- Mutual fund dealer staff guidance says "disclosure or client consent cannot be viewed as a means to justify excessive trading."

*Sources: IDPC 3402(1), (4), 3945(2)(iii); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 3.1–3.2, 3.12; 31-103CP "Switch or change transactions"; Mutual Fund Dealer Rule 200 (VI); MFDA MSN-0065*

## 1.18 Unsolicited or unsuitable client instructions

In an advisory account, suitability applies to client instructions as well as recommendations. Marking an order "unsolicited" is not enough, and the RR need not accept an unsuitable order.

> **Key rule:** The firm may carry out an unsuitable retail instruction only if it has (IDPC 3402(5)):
> 1. "informed the retail client of the basis for the determination that the action will not satisfy subsection 3402(1) and advised the client against proceeding";
> 2. recommended a suitable alternative;
> 3. "received recorded confirmation of the retail client's instruction to proceed".

| Situation | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Advisory, client's idea, suitable | Proceed; the determination is still made and documented |
| Advisory, client's idea, unsuitable | Explain, advise against, offer an alternative, get recorded confirmation, or decline |
| OEO account | No recommendations and no trade suitability; only continued account suitability (3402(3)(i), 3404(1)) |
| Signs of exploitation or incapacity | TCP and temporary hold (1.6), not 3402(5) |

**The firm's side.** Supervision must detect unsuitable trading (3945(2)(i)), and the firm answers for its RRs (1.27).

*Sources: IDPC 3402(3), (5), 3404(1), 3945(2); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 3.4.3, 3.10; GN-3200-26-001 s. 2.1*

## 1.19 Types of investment action

Suitability applies before the firm "purchases, sells, withdraws, exchanges or transfers-out securities" or "takes any other investment action" (IDPC 3402(1)). Guidance adds **depositing** and a **decision to hold**.

| Action | Suitability point |
|---|---|
| **Purchase** | KYC fit, concentration, liquidity, costs, alternatives |
| **Sale** | Client interest first, including tax and costs; liquidating transferred-in holdings is covered too |
| **Hold** | Deciding to keep a position after review is an investment action, as is leaving cash uninvested |
| **Deposit / transfer-in** | A trigger (3402(2)(i)). If suitability cannot be completed first, restrict the account to liquidating trades, transfers or disbursements |
| **Exchange / switch** | Costs, incentives and tax disclosed first (1.17) |
| **Transfer-out / withdrawal** | Consider the effect on what remains |

Pre-authorized plans are assessed at set-up and on triggers, not before each instalment.

*Sources: IDPC 3402(1)–(2); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 3.1.2, 3.4.1–3.4.4*

## 1.20 Account appropriateness vs suitability determination

| | Account appropriateness (IDPC 3211) | Suitability (IDPC 3402) |
|---|---|---|
| When | **Once, before** opening | Before each investment action, on triggers and on periodic review |
| Question | Is this account, and the access it gives, appropriate for this person? | Is this action, and the resulting portfolio, suitable, and does it put the client first? |
| Alternatives | Account types at the firm | Reasonable range of actions at the firm |
| OEO | Only whether opening is appropriate | Only whether keeping the account remains suitable |

**The link.** Guidance calls appropriateness "a one-time obligation" that the suitability obligation "tracks" after opening. Under 3402(3), the firm must keep determining that the account and the scope of access remain suitable. It reassesses the relationship when a trigger warrants, not at every review.

**Analyzing scenarios.**
- A retiree in a fee-based account who now trades once a year: **account** question.
- A request to buy a speculative stock: **trade** question.
- An OEO client asking what to buy: **account** red flag, because the client expects advice.

*Sources: IDPC 3211, 3402(3); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 3.3.1–3.3.2, 3.4.4*

## 1.21 Monitoring and maintaining accounts; trigger events

> **Remember:** Review the account and holdings within a reasonable time after (IDPC 3402(2)):
> 1. a deposit or transfer-in of securities;
> 2. a new RR, Portfolio Manager or Associate Portfolio Manager on the account;
> 3. a change in the client's KYC information that could make the account or a holding unsuitable;
> 4. a change in a security that could make it unsuitable;
> 5. the periodic KYC review (36 months; 12 for managed and discretionary).

**Extent of the review.** It depends on the type of event, the client relationship, the firm's services and the securities held. After a significant KYC change such as a job loss, consider restricting the account to liquidating trades, transfers or disbursements until the KYC is re-evaluated.

**Firm supervision.** Policies for daily and monthly retail supervision "must specifically address the detection of", among other things (IDPC 3945(2)):
- unsuitable trading;
- undue concentration;
- excessive trading;
- trading in restricted securities;
- RR–client trading conflicts;
- excessive transfers and trade cancellations indicating possible unauthorized trading;
- inappropriate or high-risk trading strategies;
- deterioration in the quality of client holdings.

> **Exam trap:** Significant market fluctuations are **not** a mandatory trigger. The RDI must state whether the firm will review suitability "in the event of significant market fluctuations" (3216(5)(ii)(d)(IV)).

*Sources: IDPC 3209, 3216(5)(ii)(d), 3402(2), 3945(2); GN-3400-21-004 ss. 2.11.2, 3.4.4*

## 1.22 Impact of changes on the suitability determination

| Change | Effect | Hook |
|---|---|---|
| **Material change in client circumstances** (job loss, inheritance, divorce, retirement, illness, a move) | Shifts capacity, horizon, liquidity or objectives | KYC trigger (3402(2)(iii)); update KYC (3209(3)) |
| **Product research** (downgrade, new manager or mandate, higher fees, merger, suspended redemptions) | A holding may no longer fit the profile | Security trigger (3402(2)(iv)); PDD monitoring (3301(1)(iii)) |
| **Market research** (a sector shock) | Concentration risk | Sector-specific change may need reassessment |
| **Economic, political, social events** (concept): rates, recession, elections, trade disputes, pandemics | Changes asset-class risk and return; can hit income or liquidity | Reassess when it changes a client's circumstances or a security |
| **Emerging issues and trends** (concept): demographics, technology, climate transition, inflation | Long-run assumptions and ESG preferences shift | Periodic review |

**Analyze in three steps.**
1. What changed: the client, the product or the environment?
2. Which KYC factor or holding does it affect?
3. Does the resulting portfolio still meet 3402(1)?

CIRO's product guidance says a general market or economic change usually does not require reassessing individual securities.

*Sources: IDPC 3209(3), 3301(1)(iii), 3402(2); GN-3300-21-001; GN-3400-21-004 s. 2.11.2; standard finance concept (verified)*

## 1.23 Managing conflicts of interest

| Step | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Firm and Approved Person identify existing and reasonably foreseeable material conflicts; the Approved Person "must promptly report that conflict of interest to the Dealer Member" (IDPC 3110) |
| **Address** | Address all material conflicts in the **client's best interest** (3111(1), 3112(1)) |
| **Avoid** | Avoid any conflict that cannot be addressed in the client's best interest (3111(2), 3112(2)). An Approved Person may not act on a conflict they identified until it is addressed **and** the firm consents (3111(3)) |
| **Disclose** | In writing, where a reasonable client would expect it. Covers the nature and extent of the conflict, its impact and risk, and how it is addressed. Must be prominent, specific and in plain language, given before opening or in a timely manner (3113) |

> **Key rule:** "A Dealer Member and an Approved Person do not satisfy subsections 3111(1) or 3112(1) solely by providing disclosure to the client." (IDPC 3113(3))

**Typical situations.**
- **Compensation incentives:** use neutral grids and monitoring.
- **Third-party compensation and proprietary products:** CSA and CIRO staff say these are almost always material, so compare the shelf with non-proprietary alternatives.
- **Gifts:** only non-monetary, minimal-value and infrequent ones (3115).
- **Specific bans:**
  - gratuities to a client's employees without the client's prior written consent (3116);
  - non-cash mutual fund sales incentives, subject to listed exceptions such as a firm-wide internal incentive programme covering all products, trailing commissions and normal business promotion (3117(3));
  - **tied selling**, though relationship pricing is allowed (3118).

> **Exam trap:** Disclosure is the floor, not the cure.

*Sources: IDPC 3110–3113, 3115(2)(i), 3116–3118; CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-363; GN-3100-21-001*

## 1.24 Outside activities

**Definition.** CIRO guidance defines outside activities as any activities conducted outside the dealer, "whether or not the Approved Person receives compensation for such activities." Examples include directorships, a family business, insurance sales, teaching and volunteer roles.

**Rule (IDPC 2554).** The activity must not be contrary to securities laws or CIRO requirements and must not bring the industry into disrepute. The Approved Person:
1. informs the firm;
2. obtains its **prior approval**.

The firm's policies must address continuous client service and conflicts, and the firm notifies CIRO under NI 33-109.

**Approval considerations.**
- Time demands on client service.
- Positions (for example with public issuers) that could prevent unbiased advice.
- **No use of client information.**
- **No use of firm premises, logos, staff or email.**
- Risk of client confusion and the reputation of others involved.

An Approved Person never approves their own request. Changes are reported within 30 days.

**Disclosure and positions of influence.** Outside roles that give the Approved Person a **position of influence** over clients must be disclosed. Examples are a religious leader, doctor, teacher at a degree-granting institution or lawyer. Trading or advising for people susceptible to that influence is prohibited.

> **Exam trap:** Approval must come **before** the activity starts.

*Sources: IDPC 2554; GN-2500-22-001*

## 1.25 Personal financial dealings with clients

> **Key rule:** "An employee or Approved Person of a Dealer Member must not, directly or indirectly, engage in any personal financial dealings with clients." (IDPC 3115(1)) The listed dealings "include, but are not limited to" the following.

| Dealing | Prohibited | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| **Accepting consideration** | Any pay, gratuity or benefit from anyone but the firm for client activities | Non-monetary, minimal and infrequent; or pay for an **approved outside activity** |
| **Settlement agreements** | Settling with a client, or paying client losses personally | With the firm's **prior written consent** (7.12) |
| **Borrowing from clients** | Borrowing or receiving a guarantee | Lending institution in its normal course; or a **Related Person** (Income Tax Act)† |
| **Lending to clients** | Lending or guaranteeing | Related Person† |
| **Control or authority** | POA, trustee, executor or other control over a client's finances | Related Person†; or within a discretionary or managed account agreement |

† The Related Person arrangement must be handled under the firm's policies and procedures. For RRs, IRs, Portfolio Managers and Associate Portfolio Managers, it must also be disclosed to and approved in writing by the firm before the transaction or arrangement (3115(2)(iii)–(v)).

**Commingling, business partnerships and investment clubs.** IDPC 3115 does not name these. Because its list is not exhaustive, mixing an Approved Person's money or assets with a client's falls under the general prohibition; this is an interpretation, not a separate rule. MFDA-era staff guidance says investment clubs (the representative and clients investing together, with the representative deciding) and co-investment schemes should be prohibited. A partner, shareholder or officer role in a client's business may be possible as an **approved outside activity**.

> **Exam trap:** A loan from an unrelated client, acting as a client's executor, and quietly reimbursing a client's loss are **all** prohibited unless the conditions above are met.

*Sources: IDPC 3115; MFDA MSN-0047*

## 1.26 Conflict-of-interest policies and procedures

The firm's policies "must specifically address identifying, disclosing and avoiding or otherwise addressing material conflict of interest situations" (IDPC 3114). The firm must also supervise how its Approved Persons address conflicts (3112(3)).

**Effective controls and qualified supervision.** Approval should only take place "where there are effective controls and qualified supervision" (outside-activities guidance). Per CSA/CIRO staff, policies cover:
- a definition of conflicts;
- responsibilities;
- escalation;
- materiality criteria;
- controls and how they are tested;
- training;
- client disclosure;
- a review at least annually.

**Due diligence for approvals.** Approval processes must be robust and impartial, and firms "must be able to provide evidence of the due diligence performed."

**Record-keeping.** Records must demonstrate conflicts compliance and document sales practices, compensation arrangements and incentive practices (IDPC 3804(2)(xvi)–(xvii)). Staff expect:
- a **conflicts inventory** recording each conflict, its materiality, impact, controls and disclosure;
- evidence of periodic reviews;
- training records.

*Sources: IDPC 3112(3), 3114, 3804(2)(xvi)–(xvii); GN-2500-22-001; CSA/CIRO Staff Notice 31-363*

## 1.27 CIRO standards of conduct

> **Key rule:** A Regulated Person "in the transaction of business must observe high standards of ethics and conduct and must act openly and fairly and in accordance with just and equitable principles of trade," and "must not engage in any business conduct that is unbecoming or detrimental to the public interest" (IDPC 1402(1)).

Conduct may breach the standard if it (1402(2)):
- is **negligent**;
- breaches a legal, regulatory, contractual or **firm-policy** obligation;
- departs unreasonably from expected standards;
- is likely to diminish investor confidence.

**Responsibility.** The firm answers for all acts and omissions of its employees, partners, Directors and officers. An Approved Person must avoid causing the firm to breach CIRO requirements (1403). The firm keeps client business within ethical bounds and good business practice (3102).

| Relationship | Breaches |
|---|---|
| **RR–client** | Unauthorized trades; signing forms for a client; misstating risk or fees; using client money or information personally; steering risk-questionnaire answers; promising returns |
| **RR–firm** | Off-book trading; falsifying KYC or records; hiding complaints, conflicts or outside activities; ignoring firm policies; misleading supervisors or CIRO |

> **Exam trap:** A breach need not be intentional or illegal. Negligence or breaking firm policy can be enough.

*Sources: IDPC 1402, 1403, 3102; CIRO Complaints Brochure*
